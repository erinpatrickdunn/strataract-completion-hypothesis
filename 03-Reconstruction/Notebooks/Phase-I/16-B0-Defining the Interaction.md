## B0.1 — What is the actual interaction?

**[C1]** The action, as frozen, contains exactly one quartic term: $-\frac{\lambda}{4}(\bar\psi\psi)^2 = -\frac{\lambda}{4}\eta^2$. Foundation A's closed Fierz system shows this single operator is algebraically identical to
$$-\frac{\lambda}{4}\eta^2 = \frac{\lambda}{20}\tilde P^2 + \frac{\lambda}{20}V^2 - \frac{\lambda}{20}A^2 + \frac{\lambda}{40}T^2$$
**These are not two interactions. They are one operator written two ways.** $S_{\rm geo}$ does not contain independent couplings $\lambda_S,\lambda_P,\lambda_V,\lambda_A,\lambda_T$ — it contains one $\lambda$, and Fierz fixes the ratios among the five representations exactly. This matters for B0.2 below.

## B0.2 — Does a single auxiliary field suffice?

**[C2] — Yes, exactly, as an identity on the path integral.** The Gaussian HS completion of the previous notebook,
$$\exp\Big\{-i\int\frac{\lambda}{4}\eta^2\Big\} = \mathcal N^{-1}\int\mathcal D\sigma\,\exp\Big\{i\int\big[\tfrac1\lambda\sigma^2-\sigma\eta\big]\Big\}$$
is an exact Gaussian identity in the single real variable $\eta$. It makes no reference to Fierz, and holds regardless of how $\eta^2$ might alternatively be written. **So the single-field HS transform of the exact quantum path integral is not in question and needs no revision.**

**[C2] — But this is not the whole story, and here is where I want to flag something the previous handoff got wrong.** I claimed in the Foundation A closure that Foundation B owed a redo of the determinant "with the full [Fierz] content." That claim conflated two different things: the *exact* path integral (unambiguous, single field suffices, per above) and the **mean-field/one-loop approximation to it** (which is what Target 0 actually computes — fermions integrated out exactly at *fixed classical* $\sigma$, with no $\sigma$-fluctuation loop included). Those are not the same object, and the distinction is the entire content of a well-documented phenomenon in the four-fermion EFT literature called the **Fierz ambiguity**.

**[C1, imported]** The Fierz ambiguity: mean-field treatments of a four-fermion interaction are, in general, *not* invariant under which Fierz-equivalent channel is chosen for the Hubbard–Stratonovich decoupling, even though the exact quantum theory is invariant, because the large-Nf phase diagram becomes highly sensitive to unphysical parameters controlling which representation of the interaction term is used, even though all decoupling schemes are completely equivalent if the model is solved rigorously. The effect is traced specifically to the **omission of Hubbard-Stratonovich-field (bosonic) fluctuations** in the mean-field truncation— incorporating those fluctuations on top of mean-field reduces the ambiguity's effect. This is precisely the approximation order Target 0 is scoped to (§4: one-loop in fermions, no boson-fluctuation corrections).

**[C1, imported — directly on point]** A companion analysis constructs the toy model most relevant here: an NJL-type single-fermion-species interaction and shows explicitly how a **Fierz parameter** interpolating between equivalent channel representations shifts which mean-field condensate forms, using a Lagrangian built from exactly the scalar, pseudoscalar, vector, and axial four-fermion channels this reconstruction has been working with. Most directly relevant: a supergravity/gravitino-condensation paper linearizes an interaction of **exactly the form $\frac14(\bar\psi\psi)^2$** — matching $S_{\rm geo}$'s quartic term structurally — and states plainly that Fierz transformations make the scalar-channel coupling ambiguous, since the interaction could equally be transformed into a pseudovector or pseudoscalar channel before linearizing, potentially yielding no scalar condensate at all, or different condensates entirely.

**Conclusion of B0.2:** A single auxiliary field is *sufficient* to represent the interaction exactly. It is **not sufficient to make the resulting one-loop/mean-field effective potential unambiguous**. The choice to HS-transform via $\sigma\leftrightarrow\eta$ specifically (rather than via $\pi\leftrightarrow\tilde P$, or some parametrized mixture) is itself an unlabeled physical choice — a second, independent Category-3 hazard in the original Target-0 derivation, structurally analogous to but distinct from the UV-regulator hazard already flagged in Target 0 §5. It was not visible before Foundation A closed, because Foundation A is what established that $\eta^2$ *has* Fierz-equivalent representations at all.

## B0.3 — Admissible backgrounds (independent question, addressed as instructed)

**[C2]** On the homogeneous, isotropic cosmological background used throughout the corpus (CT-viii, §P.9.3): spatial vector, spatial axial-vector, and tensor expectation values with a free spatial index are forced to vanish by the $SO(3)$ isotropy argument already used and derived for $A^i=0$ in §P.9.3 — the identical argument applies to $\langle V^i\rangle$ and $\langle T^{ij}\rangle,\langle T^{0i}\rangle$ (any bilinear with an uncontracted spatial index breaks the assumed isotropy of the background). $\langle V^0\rangle, \langle T^{ij}\rangle_{\text{fully contracted}}$ — wait, $T^{\mu\nu}$ is antisymmetric so it has no fully-contracted scalar piece; every component of $T^{\mu\nu}$ carries at least one free Lorentz index, and by the same argument all of $\langle T^{\mu\nu}\rangle=0$ on this background. $\langle V^0\rangle$ survives isotropy (it's a scalar under spatial rotations) but is exactly $J^0$, already governed by its own separate conservation law (§P.9.5.4) and not part of the quartic vertex's mean-field question. $\langle A^0\rangle$ survives isotropy — this is precisely CT-viii's own admitted Branch-2 possibility.

**So on this specific background, the admissible non-vanishing bilinears reduce to exactly $\{\eta,\ \langle A^0\rangle\}$** (using the real-field convention: $\tilde P$ is a Lorentz pseudoscalar and survives isotropy too, in principle, alongside $\eta$ — nothing in the isotropy argument alone forces $\tilde P=0$; that requires the *parity* argument from Foundation A §A2 instead, which does force it in a parity-symmetric vacuum). **This narrows the Fierz-ambiguity question considerably**: on this background, only the $\eta$–$\tilde P$ sub-block (plus $A^0$, already separately tracked in §P.7.7's cosmological system) is in play — $V,T$ are eliminated by symmetry before the ambiguity question even needs to be asked about them.
