## The sharper B1 question: is "set $V,A,T\to0$" exact, or a truncation?

Your question is precise: symmetry constrains the *vacuum expectation values*; it says nothing a priori about whether restricting the auxiliary-field *representation* to $V=A=T=0$ before extremizing could miss a genuine saddle point that only exists in the larger space.

**[C1, imported]** This is exactly the question Palais' Principle of Symmetric Criticality (PSC) answers: if a variational principle is invariant under a symmetry group G, a symmetric field configuration is an extremal of the full (unrestricted) functional if and only if it is an extremal of the functional restricted to symmetric configurations — with the theorem's validity established rigorouslywhen G is a group of Riemannian isometries, when G is compact, or (with some restrictions) when G is semi-simple. Our case — $SO(3)$ acting linearly on the auxiliary fields $V^\mu,A^\mu,T^{\mu\nu}$ by rotation, with the fermion determinant manifestly $SO(3)$-covariant since it's built by covariant contraction of these fields with $\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\mu\gamma^5,\sigma^{\mu\nu}$ — is precisely the setting the theorem targets: $SO(3)$ compact, action linear, invariant functional. **Under the theorem, restricting to $V=A=T=0$ (mod the $u^\mu$-aligned pieces) before extremizing is not a truncation: it is guaranteed to recover every symmetric critical point that the full, unrestricted extremization would have found.**

**[C1, imported — the caveat I will not paper over.** The same literature source flags explicitlythat the principle need not be well-defined and, even when defined, need not be valid — citing specifically Hawking's finding that it fails for spatially homogeneous cosmological models. This is a direct hit on the exact setting we're in (an FLRW-type homogeneous background), and I am not in a position to wave it away. Hawking's counterexample (in the minisuperspace/Bianchi-cosmology reduction literature) concerns cases where the symmetric reduction of the *field equations* loses genuine constraint content present in the unrestricted variation — the reduced (symmetric-only) Euler–Lagrange equations are not always equivalent to the restriction of the full Euler–Lagrange equations when the symmetry group's orbit structure interacts badly with the constraint structure of the theory.

**Honest status of the B1 question:** PSC's general conditions ($SO(3)$ compact, linear action) are satisfied at the level of the *field content* here, which is encouraging and is a real, positive result — stronger than the symmetry argument alone, since it addresses your representation-vs-solution distinction directly rather than assuming it away. But I have **not** checked whether our specific setup falls into the Hawking-type exception, which is specifically a cosmological/constrained-system phenomenon, and our background (§P.9's FLRW ansatz with its own Hamiltonian constraint structure, per §P.9.5.1) is exactly the kind of constrained system where that exception was found to bite.
