### The Scope Principle

**Statement.** Every mathematical conclusion produced during a reconstruction is scoped to the explicit construction from which it was derived. Extending that conclusion to different bosonizations, effective actions, approximation schemes, or downstream corpus documents requires a separate derivation, not analogy.

### Rationale

A result proven for one specific construction (a specific HS decoupling, a specific loop order, a specific regulator) does not automatically transfer to a structurally similar but distinct construction. The failure mode this principle targets is the unstated leap from "shown for construction $X$" to "true in general" — precisely the move that produced two documented overreaches during this reconstruction (below).

### Case History

**First instance.** `Foundations Notebook 2` derived one Fierz identity ($\eta^2$ in terms of $P^2,V^2,A^2,T^2$) and momentarily treated it as establishing the full operator basis, before the involution check (`A4a`) revealed the check itself had been performed on a truncated sub-block rather than the full sixteen-dimensional swap operator. Correcting this required explicitly separating "the swap operator $P^2=\mathbb1$ trivially" from "the induced map on a five-channel truncation," which do not have the same proof.

**Second instance.** During Foundation C0, a finding that the §P.11.2 quartic term did not arise in one constructed Hubbard–Stratonovich bosonization was stated as "cannot originate from Object B at all" — an unbounded claim about all possible exact bosonizations (bilocal fields, CJT/2PI, different fermion-integration orders) based on checking exactly one. Corrected to: "does not arise within the explicit Object-B construction carried out in this reconstruction."

### Application Rule

Before generalizing any result beyond the construction that produced it, state explicitly: (a) what was constructed, (b) what was shown for it, (c) whether the generalization requires a new derivation. If (c) has not been done, the generalized statement may not appear as an established conclusion — only as a flagged, open extension.
