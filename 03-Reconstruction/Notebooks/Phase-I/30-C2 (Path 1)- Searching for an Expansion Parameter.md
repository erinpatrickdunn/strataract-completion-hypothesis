# C2 (Path 1): Searching for an Expansion Parameter in $S_{\rm geo}$

*Adopting the sharper framing: not "derive a control criterion" but "what is the expansion parameter, does it exist, and where would it first enter."*

## Candidate parameters, checked systematically against the frozen action

**[C1]** $S_{\rm geo}$'s field content (Constitution §2): one Dirac spinor $\psi$, no internal indices, no flavor or color multiplicity. Parameters appearing in the action: $m$ (mass), $\lambda$ (quartic coupling). That's the entire parameter space — two dimensionful/dimensionless constants, no integer multiplicity, no separate small coupling distinct from $\lambda$ itself.

**Candidate 1 — Large $N$.** [C2] Ruled out directly: a $1/N$ expansion requires $N$ copies of the fermion field with an $O(N)$ or $U(N)$-type internal symmetry, so that boson loops (which involve a fermion loop themselves, in the HS construction) are suppressed by an explicit power of $1/N$ relative to the leading fermion determinant. $S_{\rm geo}$ has $N=1$ by construction (Constitution §2, explicitly: "the only fundamental dynamical field"). **No such parameter is present, and none could be introduced without literally changing $S_{\rm geo}$ — this isn't a documentation gap, it's a structural absence in the frozen action itself.**

**Candidate 2 — Weak coupling in $\lambda$.** [C2] Checked directly against the actual functional dependence established in B1/B2: the boson quadratic term scales as $1/\lambda$ (from the HS completion, $\sigma^2/(\lambda(1-t))$ etc.), while the fermion-loop term $F(M^2)$ has no explicit $\lambda$-dependence at all except through the saddle-point value $\sigma_0$ itself. So the *ratio* of a would-be boson-loop correction to the kept fermion-loop term does not organize into powers of $\lambda$ in the way a standard weak-coupling loop expansion would (where each additional loop costs one explicit power of the coupling). **This candidate fails on inspection of the actual functional form already derived — not on general principle, but on the specific structure of $\Gamma_t(\sigma,\pi)$ constructed in B1/B2.**

**Candidate 3 — Semiclassical/$\hbar$ expansion.** [C1, standard] In a genuine $\hbar$-expansion, the classical (tree) action is $O(\hbar^0)$, the one-loop determinant is $O(\hbar^1)$, and a boson loop built on top of the saddle is $O(\hbar^2)$ — this is the standard justification for stopping at one fermion loop in ordinary QFT mean-field treatments, and it's worth taking seriously here because it doesn't require any extra structure ($N$, weak coupling) beyond ordinary path-integral bookkeeping.

**[C2] — checking whether this actually applies to $S_{\rm geo}$ as constructed.** Restoring $\hbar$ explicitly in the path integral, $Z=\int\mathcal D\psi\mathcal D\bar\psi\,e^{\frac i\hbar S_{\rm geo}}$. The HS auxiliary field, introduced via a Gaussian integral over $\sigma$, does **not** carry an independent $\hbar$-suppression relative to the fermion loop in the standard way this works in, e.g., large-$N$ or genuinely weakly-coupled scalar theories — because $\sigma$ here is not an independent propagating degree of freedom with its own kinetic term at tree level (Foundation B1.3: no kinetic term for $\sigma$, only an algebraic/mass-like term). **[C2, the actual finding]:** in a standard $\hbar$-counting, each *vertex* contributes one inverse power of $\hbar$ and each *loop* (propagator loop) contributes one power of $\hbar$; the fermion loop in $\mathrm{Tr}\ln D$ is $O(\hbar)$ relative to tree, exactly as usual — **but a "boson loop" here would mean integrating out fluctuations of $\sigma$ around its saddle, and because $\sigma$'s entire role is to be a Lagrange-multiplier-like stand-in for the exactly-eliminable fermion bilinear (not an independent field with its own $\hbar$-suppressed kinetic term), the standard power-counting argument that boson loops are higher order in $\hbar$ than the fermion loop does not straightforwardly apply.** This needs to be stated carefully: in genuine NJL-type large-$N$ constructions, this exact issue is resolved by the large-$N$ counting (Candidate 1), not by $\hbar$-counting alone — the auxiliary field's fluctuations are suppressed by $1/N$, not by $\hbar$. Since $S_{\rm geo}$ has no $N$ (established above), the usual mechanism that would make Candidate 3 work is **absent for exactly the same structural reason Candidate 1 failed.**

**Candidate 4 — Derivative/gradient expansion (slowly varying fields).** [C2] This organizes an expansion in powers of $\partial_\mu\sigma/\Lambda$ or similar — relevant to whether *spatial/temporal variation* of the condensate can be neglected, not to whether *boson loops* can be neglected. It's a different expansion, addressing a different approximation (already implicitly used in restricting to spatially homogeneous backgrounds, §P.9.3) — but it doesn't bear on the fermion-loop/boson-loop ordering question at all. **Not applicable to the specific gap Branch 2 identified**, though worth noting as a separate, already-implicitly-used approximation elsewhere in the corpus that hasn't itself been separately audited.

**Candidate 5 — Dilute/low-density expansion.** [C1, imported, directly relevant] This is exactly what Foundation A's own closed material already uses and names: the density hierarchy $\varepsilon(\rho)=\rho/\rho_c\le10^{-23}$ at galactic densities (Appendix P §P.2a), explicitly invoked to suppress *Term 3* (the quartic torsion contribution) relative to Term 2. **This is a genuine, already-present small parameter in the corpus** — but note carefully, per the Scope Principle, exactly what it controls: it suppresses higher-order-in-$\psi$ corrections to $Q_{\mu\nu}$ (the uniqueness argument of Theorem 1), **not** boson loops in the condensate's own effective potential. It's the right kind of object (a genuine, stated, dimensionless small parameter derived from physical density ratios) but doesn't answer the specific question C1 raised about the fermion-loop/boson-loop truncation in Target 0's flat-space vacuum calculation, which concerns the condensate's own self-interaction, evaluated at whatever density makes $\eta_{\rm eq}\ne0$ in the first place — not the galactic-density regime $\varepsilon(\rho)$ was built for.

## Where the assumption first enters, and the honest finding

**[C2]** Tracing back through B1/B2/C0: the fermion-loop-only truncation was inherited from the *original* Target-0 Constitution (§5(e): "evaluated to one-loop order in the fermion determinant... via the standard Coleman-Weinberg-type effective potential construction for a Yukawa-coupled fermion"). **This was stated as the definition of Target 0's scope from the very first document in this reconstruction — it was a scoping choice made explicitly as a Category-1 definitional restriction, not derived as a controlled approximation and never claimed to be one.** Rereading the original Constitution's own language confirms this: §4 explicitly lists finite-temperature corrections, cosmological backgrounds, and nonequilibrium evolution as "excluded... deferred to later, separately-scoped targets" — boson-loop corrections were never listed as excluded-because-small; they were simply outside Target 0's originally stated scope by definition.

**This changes the finding materially.** C1 asked whether a control criterion is *documented*. Tracing the actual origin shows something sharper: **Target 0 never claimed the fermion-loop/no-boson-loop truncation was a controlled approximation at all — it was stated from the outset as the definition of what Target 0 computes, with boson-loop corrections explicitly out of scope by construction, not neglected as small.** The apparent "gap" C1 found is not a missing justification for an approximation; it's the natural consequence of Target 0 having been scoped, correctly and explicitly, to compute something narrower than the full effective potential from the start.

**Consequence for Foundation B's Fierz-ambiguity result:** this reframes, but does not weaken, B2's finding. B2 showed that *Target-0's object* (fermion-loop-only, scoped as such from the beginning) is representation-dependent. The finding now is: **this was never advertised as the full story**, so the representation-dependence isn't a failure of an approximation that was supposed to be controlled — it's a demonstrated property of a deliberately narrow, well-defined quantity (the Category-1-scoped Target-0 potential), which simply turns out not to be Fierz-invariant, and whether the *full* theory (including whatever Target 0 excluded) is Fierz-invariant remains genuinely open, exactly as B2 v1.2 already stated.

## Summary answer to the four questions posed

1. **What small parameter is assumed?** None — checked against five standard candidates ($N$, weak coupling, $\hbar$, gradient expansion, density hierarchy), none apply to the specific fermion-loop/boson-loop ordering question.
2. **Where does the assumption first enter?** In the original Constitution's §5(e), as an explicit scoping *definition*, not a derived approximation.
3. **Does neglecting boson loops follow from it?** No — because it was never claimed to follow from anything; it was the stated boundary of Target 0's object from the start.
4. **Is there another organizing principle?** None identified among the standard candidates checked here. This is a genuine negative finding, not an unexamined gap — five specific candidates were checked against $S_{\rm geo}$'s actual, stated content and each failed for a specific, stated structural reason.
