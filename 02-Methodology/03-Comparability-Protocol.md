### The Comparability Protocol

**Statement.** Before comparing two constructions, answer three questions for each, independently, in writing, before setting them side by side:

1. What object does this construction compute?
2. What approximations or definitions are built in?
3. What is the overlapping domain, if any, on which comparison is licensed?

Agreement or disagreement between two constructions may only be interpreted as a physical finding after these three questions are answered for both. Prior to that, a disagreement may simply reflect that the two constructions compute different things.

### Rationale

This is an operational corollary of the Scope Principle, specific to the situation where two independently-derived objects are being set against each other (rather than one construction being checked against corpus documentation). This is a qualitatively different risk than single-construction auditing: the temptation to read a numerical or structural mismatch between two constructions as "the theory is inconsistent," when it may only mean the two constructions were never commensurable, is stronger than the risk addressed by the Scope Principle alone.

### Anticipated Use

This protocol governs all Phase II work by design (`Reconstruction_Master_Report_v1.md`, Part I.4; consolidated monograph Chapter 1). Any construction proposed in `Notebooks/Phase-II/Alternative-Constructions/` (e.g., a large-$N$ generalization or CJT/2PI construction of the SCH fermion sector) must have its scope stated per this protocol before being compared against the Phase-I fermion-sector reconstruction (`03-Reconstruction/Reconstruction_Master_Report_v1.md`, Part IV).

### Application Rule

No comparison table, figure, or conclusion in the project may present two constructions' results side by side without a preceding statement addressing all three questions above for each construction.
