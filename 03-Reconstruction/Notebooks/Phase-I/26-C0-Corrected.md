# C0 — Corrected

You're right, and I want to flag exactly what happened: I made a universal negative claim ("cannot originate from Object B at all") on the basis of a single constructed instance — precisely the move this whole reconstruction has been designed to catch when other people did it (Theorem 6 asserting a potential form without deriving it; §P.11.2 introducing a symmetric completion without justification). Doing it here about the Fierz-completed potential's provenance would be the same error in a new location. Corrected below.

## The overreaching claim, replaced

Struck: *"...the quartic $(\eta^2+P^2)^2$ piece cannot originate from Object B at all under the construction given here..."*

Replaced with, exactly as proposed: **The quartic $(\eta^2+P^2)^2$ interaction is not produced by the explicit HS construction documented in this reconstruction prior to any bosonic-loop approximation. Consequently, its provenance within the current documented derivation remains unidentified.**

This is the correct scope. I constructed *one* exact bosonization (the standard single-pass HS transform, generalized across the five-channel coefficient family in C0's Object B). I did not — and cannot, from that alone — rule out that a bilocal auxiliary field construction, a 2PI/CJT effective action, or fermions integrated out in a different order might generate an effective quartic self-interaction among the auxiliary fields even *before* any boson-loop truncation is applied. That would require its own impossibility argument, which I have not attempted and should not claim by implication.

## Adding the Provenance Theorem

### Provenance Theorem (C0)

For the explicit derivation constructed in Foundations A–C:
$$A \longrightarrow B \longrightarrow C$$
every approximation and truncation is documented through the mean-field fermion determinant. The derivation halts only at the unresolved UV regularization step (the original Target-0 obstruction). Theorem 6 and §P.11 currently do not contain a documented derivation establishing that their effective potentials arise from the same A→B→C chain.

This is the correct level of claim: it specifies the exact chain being compared against, and states a documentation fact (absence of a shown derivation) rather than an impossibility claim (absence of any possible derivation). It's also, on reflection, the more useful statement for anyone revisiting Theorem 6 or §P.11 later — it tells them precisely what chain their potential would need to be checked against, rather than asserting in advance that no such check could succeed.
