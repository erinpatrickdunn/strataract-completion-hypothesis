# 03-Reconstruction

## Purpose

This directory applies the methodology defined in `02-Methodology/` to recover, analyze, and audit the existing SCH corpus.

Reconstruction is a **descriptive** process rather than a **revisionary** one. Its role is to determine what the current corpus actually derives, documents, and supports—not what the theory ought to say.

## Reconstruction Firewall

Reconstruction operates under the **Reconstruction Firewall** defined by the Governance Charter.

Accordingly, reconstruction documents:

* recover derivation chains,
* identify undocumented assumptions,
* classify claim status,
* trace dependency relationships,
* record inconsistencies,
* document unresolved questions.

They do **not**:

* rewrite canonical theory,
* silently replace equations,
* introduce new physical mechanisms,
* promote alternative constructions,
* alter historical documents.

Any proposed scientific correction belongs in `05-Alternatives/` until formally adopted through project governance.

## Relationship to Canonical Theory

Reconstruction findings are **about** the canonical theory.

They are not themselves canonical theory.

For example, a reconstruction may conclude that:

> A derivation is undocumented.

This is a finding about the corpus, not a revision of the underlying scientific claim.

Similarly, identifying an inconsistency does not automatically invalidate a result; it records the current documented state and any gaps requiring future work.

---

## Guiding Principle

Reconstruction seeks to maximize **traceability** rather than **novelty**.

Every conclusion should identify:

* the source document,
* the construction being analyzed,
* the derivation chain examined,
* the methodology applied,
* the resulting classification.

The objective is a transparent, reproducible map of the project's current scientific state.
